# A space to care for myself — English script (DRAFT for review)

Translation of «Un espacio para cuidarme» (Guion 1 in `docs/agente/guiones-cuidado-emocional-y-autoestima.md`).
Same 13 blocks, same order, same rules: one instruction at a time, everything optional, no promises.
**Not recorded yet. It needs Reyna's review before recording.**

Only the text under each block is read aloud. Titles and block numbers are never spoken.

## Block 1

Welcome to Súper Conciencia. I'm Guru, a meditation guide powered by artificial intelligence. For this time, I invite you to make space to care for yourself, without asking yourself to feel anything in particular.

If you call this moment healing, we can understand that word as an intention of emotional care. You don't need to resolve a difficulty or change how you feel in order to take part.

Find a place where you can pay attention without driving or doing anything else that needs your focus. You can stop before the end. This practice does not replace professional care.

Choose a posture that lets you feel at ease. You can keep your eyes open. Look at an object nearby and recognize the place where you are.

## Block 2

Notice how your body is supported. You may notice your feet on the floor, the chair beneath your legs, or the contact of one hand with the other.

You don't need to take any special posture. If you want to move, you can. Adjusting your position is also part of looking after yourself.

Choose a simple point of attention. It can be a sound in the room or a surface you can feel. If it is comfortable for you, you can also notice your breathing just as it happens, without making it deeper or slower.

During the next space, come back to that point whenever you need to. You don't have to hold your attention continuously. You can look around at any moment.

## Block 3

Perhaps thoughts have come up about what is still left to do. Perhaps you are wondering whether this practice is working. You don't need to answer those questions now.

If you notice that your attention has drifted, you can acknowledge it with one simple word: thinking. Then listen to a sound, or feel the support of your feet again.

This is not about pushing your thoughts away. You can let them stay there while you choose to attend to something else for a moment.

If your attention shifts many times today, you can come back many times. If you would rather pause or finish, that is fine too. Stay with the anchor that feels most comfortable to you.

## Block 4

Without looking for any story, you can ask yourself how you arrive at this moment. A word may come up: tiredness, restlessness, calm, or something you can't name.

There is no need to go deeper. You can leave the question unanswered. If observing an emotion makes you uncomfortable, turn your attention to the colors you see or the sounds you hear.

If you want to acknowledge what you notice, try a short phrase: right now there is some tiredness, or right now I notice restlessness. Use your own words.

You don't need to turn that experience into a problem you must solve in these minutes. See whether it is possible to keep yourself company without asking for an explanation.

## Block 5

You may notice some demand that is with you today. An idea of how you should feel, how much you should be able to do, or what you should already have solved.

There is no need to go into its content. You can recognize that the demand is present and return to the contact with the floor.

If it helps, listen to this possibility: for this moment, I can leave one task unsolved. You can change the phrase, or not use it.

Resting here does not require having finished everything. You don't have to prove that you deserve this pause either. You can simply decide to give a little attention to what is happening now.

Stay with one concrete, simple sensation while we let a space pass without words.

## Block 6

Notice again what is holding you. You can gently touch the fabric of your clothes, rest one hand on the other, or feel the back of the chair.

Don't look for an intense sensation. Even a small perception, like the temperature of a surface, can be enough to guide your attention.

If you feel like it, change one detail to be more at ease: loosen a piece of clothing, shift your posture, or open your eyes if they were closed. You don't need to stay still.

Notice which option you prefer in this moment. It doesn't have to be the same one you chose at the beginning. The practice can adapt to you as it happens.

Now let your attention rest for a while on that support.

## Block 7

You can try a simple way of speaking to yourself. Not a phrase meant to convince you that everything is fine, but words you can accept today.

Perhaps: I can treat myself with a little respect as I go through this day. Or perhaps: I can acknowledge that this is hard for me.

If no phrase works for you, you don't need to repeat them. Listening to the sounds around you is also a way of practicing.

If you choose some words, say them once in silence and notice how they feel. You can change them so they sound like you. Then return to your anchor, without checking whether the words produced a change.

Relief does not need to appear for you to continue or to finish.

## Block 8

For the next few moments, you can stop working with words. Notice something that is happening outside of you: a sound nearby, the light in the room, or a shape you can see.

There is no need to interpret what you perceive. Just recognize one detail and, if you want, move on to another.

If thoughts or emotions come up, you don't have to push them away. You can choose to keep attending to your surroundings, with your eyes open if that suits you better.

This pause does not ask you to forget your difficulties. It only offers you a moment of simpler attention.

Take the space that feels comfortable. If the silence feels long, you can look around, move, or stop the recording.

## Block 9

If you want to return to a question, let it be a small one: what gesture of care would be possible for me today?

You don't need to find a solution for your whole life. It may be drinking some water, eating at a time that suits you, resting for a few minutes, or asking for company. Perhaps no idea comes up today.

You can choose something concrete and realistic, without turning it into another obligation. You can also decide that you will think about it later.

If the need for human support comes up, you can acknowledge it without having to work out here how to ask for it. This recording does not need to take that place.

Return now to a sound or to the contact of your feet, while you leave the question on pause.

## Block 10

You may have noticed something different during the practice. You may feel the same, or you may prefer to finish. You can recognize any of those experiences without calling it a success or a failure.

There is no emotion you must take with you when you leave. You can go on with your day with whatever is here now, attending to what you need.

If the urge to compare this moment with another one comes up, notice that you are comparing and return to your surroundings. There is no need to turn this session into a test.

During the next space, choose your way of being here: listening, feeling a surface, looking at an object, or moving gently. You don't need to go any deeper for this time to be meaningful to you.

## Block 11

We are going to move toward the end, without hurry. Begin by noticing a little more of the place where you are.

You can recognize the distance between you and an object nearby. Listen to the sounds in the room. Feel the floor or the surface that holds you.

If your eyes are closed and you want to open them, do it whenever it suits you. If you would rather move your fingers or change the position of your legs, you can do that now.

You don't need to come out of any special state. We are simply widening attention toward the everyday.

While a few minutes remain, let your surroundings take up more space again. You can keep one simple sensation as a reference, without continuing to concentrate with effort.

## Block 12

Remember, if it helps, the gesture of care you considered earlier. You can keep it, make it smaller, or leave it for another time.

You may want to write down a word when we finish. It can be a sensation, a need, or something you would like to remember. You can also close the session without recording anything.

There is no obligation to turn this practice into a visible change. You may simply have given this time to observing and choosing how to keep yourself company.

We will now leave one last quiet space. Stay in contact with your surroundings. If you want to get up, you can wait for the closing, or stop the recording and do it at your own pace.

The decision to continue or to finish is still yours.

## Block 13

The practice is coming to an end. Look around you and recognize where you are. Notice the support of your feet and any movement you want to make.

You don't need to take an answer with you. If you wish to keep something, it can be this question: what gesture of care would be possible for me today?

You can return to your activities whenever you like, giving yourself time to settle. This session ends here.
